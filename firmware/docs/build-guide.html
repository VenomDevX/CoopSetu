Three parts to double-check

The pull-up must be 10 kΩ. Hardware.md records it as 200 Ω. GPIO34 is input-only with no internal pull-up, so this resistor is the only thing holding the pin high. At 200 Ω every press sinks 3.3 V ÷ 200 Ω ≈ 16 mA — above the ESP32's ~12 mA recommended continuous per-pin current.

LED resistors must be 220 Ω. Also recorded as 200 Ω. Less serious (≈7 mA vs ≈6 mA) but use the right part.

Is your buzzer active or passive? Test it with a 3 V coin cell — a beep means active, a single click means passive, in which case set BUZZER_IS_PASSIVE 1. If it's rated 5 V or draws over ~12 mA, do not wire it straight to GPIO32; it needs a transistor or MOSFET.

Tools

  • Soldering iron and solder
  • Breadboard for testing, perfboard for the final build
  • Jumper wires, male-to-female and male-to-male
  • Multimeter — not optional, you need it at stage 1
  • Small flat screwdriver for the PN532's DIP switches

02Before you solder

Not general advice — three specific consequences of this pin map.

The CAM's UART pins are its programming pins

Hardware.md connects CAM GPIO1 and GPIO3 to the terminal. Those are U0TXD and U0RXD — the same pins a USB-serial adapter uses to flash the board. Three consequences you cannot design around:

  • Disconnect both wires every time you flash the CAM. Leave them on and the flash fails, or half-succeeds.
  • The CAM can never print debug output. Serial is the link. Anything printed becomes protocol noise — which is why the firmware reports problems as ERR frames.
  • Every CAM reset dumps bootloader chatter into the terminal. Handled: messages are framed #<body>*<checksum> and unparseable input is dropped.
Bench tip

Put a jumper or 2-pin header on those two wires. You will disconnect them more often than you expect.

Three devices share one SPI bus, and one is backwards

GPIO18 (clock), GPIO23 (MOSI) and GPIO19 (MISO) are shared by the display, its touch controller, the display's unused microSD slot, and the PN532.

The PN532 clocks SPI LSB-first. Everything else is MSB-first. This works only because every pinned library wraps transfers in SPI.beginTransaction(), which re-applies bit order per transaction.

If you substitute a library

Verify it uses SPI transactions. The failure mode is intermittent corruption that looks exactly like a bad solder joint — you will spend hours reflowing joints that were fine.

The display's unused microSD CS (GPIO26) is driven HIGH at boot and never touched again. A floating SD CS lets the card drive MISO and corrupt every transfer on the bus. Already handled in display.cpp — just don't remove that wire.

Power budget

LoadCurrent

Use a 2 A supply. A 1 A charger appears to work, then browns out the first time a Wi-Fi transmit coincides with a camera frame — which presents as random reboots and is very hard to attribute.

The TFT, touch and PN532 all run from the devkit's on-board 3.3 V regulator, often rated only 500–800 mA. This combination has not been measured. If you see instability under load, feed the 3.3 V rail from a separate regulator.

03Wiring, stage by stage

Each stage ends in something you can measure. Tick them off as you go — progress is saved in this browser.

Rule for the whole section

Power off before every wiring change. Power on only at a checkpoint.

STAGE 1 Power only

Wire nothing but power. 5 V supply + to ESP32 VIN; 5 V supply − to ESP32 GND.

Checkpoint — measure it

Multimeter between 3V3 and GND. You must read 3.2–3.4 V. If you read 0 V or under 3 V, stop and check polarity — reversed power destroys the board.

STAGE 2 Ground and 3.3 V rails

Use a breadboard rail or solder bus — don't daisy-chain grounds through modules.

To ESP32 GND: CAM GND, ILI9341 GND, PN532 GND, buzzer −, pushbutton pin 2, both LED cathodes (short legs).

To ESP32 3V3: ILI9341 VCC, ILI9341 LED, PN532 VCC, 10 kΩ pull-up pin 2.

The CAM is the exception — it takes 5 V straight from the supply, not 3.3 V from the ESP32.

Checkpoint

3.3 V at the ILI9341 VCC and PN532 VCC pins. 5 V at the CAM's 5V pin.

STAGE 3 LEDs and buzzer

Green: GPIO21 → 220 Ω → LED anode (long leg); cathode → GND.

Red: GPIO22 → 220 Ω → LED anode; cathode → GND.

Buzzer: GPIO32 → buzzer +; buzzer − → GND.

LEDs are polarised — long leg toward the resistor. Backwards they simply don't light; no damage, easy to miss.

STAGE 4 Pushbutton and external pull-up

3.3 V → 10 kΩ → GPIO34 → button → GND. Idle, the resistor holds the pin high; pressed, the button pulls it to ground. The firmware reads active-LOW.

A 4-pin tactile switch has pins joined in pairs. If the button seems permanently pressed, you've used two from the same pair — rotate it 90°.

Checkpoint

Measure GPIO34 to ground: 3.3 V idle, 0 V pressed. Always 3.3 V means the button isn't connected; always 0 V means check the resistor.

Set the DIP switches to SPI first

The PN532's interface is chosen by two switches on the board, not by firmware. Almost every "PN532 not found" report is this. SPI = SW1 OFF, SW2 ON. (I²C is ON/OFF; HSU is OFF/OFF.) Some boards label them 0 and 1 — check the silkscreen.

05Software setup

One version choice decides whether the camera sketch compiles at all.

Arduino IDE and the ESP32 core

In Preferences → Additional Board Manager URLs, add:

https://espressif.github.io/arduino-esp32/package_esp32_index.json

Then Tools → Board → Boards Manager, search esp32, and install 2.0.17.

Install 2.0.17, not 3.x

The face recogniser (face_recognition_112_v1_s8) is an ESP-DL model bundled with the 2.0.x core. It was removed in 3.x, so CoopSetuCam will not compile there. An error reading human_face_detect_msr01.hpp: No such file or directory means you're on 3.x — use the Boards Manager version dropdown to select 2.0.17 and install again.

07Flashing both boards

The terminal is ordinary. The CAM has a specific sequence.

The terminal

Connect by USB, select the port, open CoopSetuTerminal.ino, confirm the board settings, and upload. If it stalls at Connecting........, hold the BOOT button while it connects.

The ESP32-CAM

  1. Disconnect both wires between CAM GPIO1/GPIO3 and the terminal.
  2. Wire the USB-serial adapter, set to 3.3 V: GND→GND, 5V→5V, adapter TX→U0R, adapter RX→U0T.
  3. Connect CAM GPIO0 to GND — the board only enters flash mode this way.
  4. Press RESET, then upload. Expect 1–3 minutes; the binary is large.
  5. Remove the GPIO0 jumper, press RESET, disconnect the adapter, reconnect the two terminal wires.
The most common mistake

Forgetting to remove the GPIO0 jumper. The board silently stays in bootloader mode and never runs your sketch.

08Touch calibration

Every panel differs. Do this once.

  1. Set TOUCH_CALIBRATION_MODE 1 and flash.
  2. Open the Serial Monitor at 115200.
  3. Tap each of the four corners, as close to the edge as you can.
  4. Put the extreme values into TOUCH_RAW_MIN_X / MAX_X / MIN_Y / MAX_Y.
  5. Set TOUCH_CALIBRATION_MODE 0 and reflash.

If touch comes out mirrored, swap MIN and MAX for that axis.

10Cards and faces

All administration happens over the Serial Monitor at 115200.

CommandDoes
statusWi-Fi, CAM link, session state, slot map
enrol <slot> <uuid>Bind a face slot to a trainee, then capture
forget <slot>Clear one slot — 255 clears all
sessionRe-arm the session QR

Enrolling a face

Get the trainee's UUID from the dashboard, then type enrol 0 3f8c1e22-…. The screen shows ENROL FACE; have them look at the camera while four frames are captured. Slots are 0–15, so 16 faces maximum per terminal.

Writing NFC cards

Cards carry the trainee UUID in a single NDEF Text record. Use the free NFC Tools app: Write → Add a record → Text, paste the UUID and nothing else, then hold the card to the phone.

Tapping an unwritten card shows its UID with "Card not linked", so you can tell which card still needs writing.

11Daily operation

Two steps to start a session, then it runs itself.

Starting a session

  1. The trainer opens the attendance session on the dashboard, which shows a QR code.
  2. Long-press the terminal button (~1.5 s) to enter arm mode.
  3. Hold the QR to the camera. The screen confirms "Session armed".

During the session

NFC: the trainee taps their card, and is marked automatically. Face: the trainee presses the button briefly, then looks at the camera.

Green LED with rising beeps means marked. Red LED with one long beep means rejected, with the reason on screen.

The session is held for four hours, or until the server reports the token expired — at which point the terminal drops it and asks you to re-arm, rather than failing every tap identically.

13Backend blockers

Server-side gaps, not wiring or firmware faults.

The terminal cannot post attendance yet

app/api/v1/attendance/qr/route.ts authenticates with createClient(), which reads a cookie session belonging to a signed-in human. A wall-mounted terminal has no cookie, so its requests are rejected.

The firmware already sends Authorization: Bearer <API_DEVICE_TOKEN>. The server needs a matching device-token guard that resolves to a service-role client for that route. No firmware change is needed once it exists.

Face embeddings are not interchangeable

Worth knowing before anyone tries to unify them:

SourceEmbeddingMetric
Web app (face-api.js)128-dim floatEuclidean, threshold 0.6
ESP32-CAM (ESP-DL)512-dim int8Cosine similarity

Different dimensionality, scale and distance metric, with no conversion between them. A face enrolled in the web app will never match one enrolled on the terminal, and vice versa. The firmware therefore recognises entirely on the CAM and maps a local slot to a trainee id, so the server just sees an ordinary QR check-in.

Before production

  • Set API_TLS_INSECURE to false and use https://. At true the terminal accepts any certificate, so anything on the network path can read and rewrite attendance traffic.
  • There is no offline queue. A check-in during a Wi-Fi outage is lost. Hardware.md records no SD module, and the display's slot is deliberately unused.
